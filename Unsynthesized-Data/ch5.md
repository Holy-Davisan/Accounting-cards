## tid bits
* Companies use a contra account, instead of debiting Sales Revenue, to track separately in the accounts and to report separately in the income statement the amount of sales returns and allowances. Disclosure of this information is important to management for the following reasons:

* Identifies Underlying Problems: Excessive returns and allowances may suggest issues such as inferior merchandise, inaccuracies in filling orders, errors in billing customers, or delivery and shipment mistakes.

* Preserves Transparency: A decrease (debit) recorded directly to Sales Revenue would obscure the relative importance of sales returns and allowances as a percentage of sales.

* Maintains Comparability: Recording directly to Sales Revenue could distort comparisons between total sales in different accounting periods.

Multiple-Choice QuestionQuestion:A credit sale is made on July 10 for $1,150, terms 2/15, n/30. On July 12, the customer returns $150 of goods for credit. On July 19, the seller receives the balance due within the discount period. What are the three account titles and their respective values recorded by the seller on July 19?A) Debit Cash $1,150; Credit Accounts Receivable $1,150B) Debit Cash $980, Debit Sales Discounts $20; Credit Accounts Receivable $1,000C) Debit Cash $1,000, Credit Sales Discounts $20; Credit Accounts Receivable $980D) Debit Cash $1,127, Debit Sales Discounts $23; Credit Accounts Receivable $1,150Correct Answer: BExplanationTo find the correct account titles and values, you must calculate the net balance due after the return and then apply the discount:Calculate Net Accounts Receivable: The original sale was $1,150, but the customer returned $150 worth of goods.$$\$1,150 - \$150 = \$1,000$$The customer now only owes $1,000. Therefore, Accounts Receivable must be credited for $1,000 to completely clear the remaining debt.Calculate the Discount: The terms are 2/15, and the payment is made 9 days later (within the 15-day window). The 2% discount applies only to the net amount owed:$$2\% \times \$1,000 = \$20$$This is recorded as a debit to Sales Discounts for $20.Calculate Cash Received: The cash collected is the net balance minus the discount:$$\$1,000 - \$20 = \$980$$This is recorded as a debit to Cash for $980.
