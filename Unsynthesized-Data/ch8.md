* Accounts receivable — Amounts customers owe on account.
* Receivables — Amounts due from individuals and companies expected to be collected in cash.
* Trade receivables — Notes and accounts receivable resulting from sales transactions.

### **Valuation and Disposition of Accounts Receivable**

* Aging the accounts receivable — Schedule classifying balances by length of time unpaid.
* Allowance method — Estimating uncollectible accounts at the end of each period.
* Bad Debt Expense — Expense recording losses from extending credit.
* Cash (net) realizable value — Net cash expected from receivables.
* Direct write-off method — Writing off receivables when deemed uncollectible.
* Factor — Finance company that buys receivables and collects payments.
* Percentage-of-receivables basis — Estimating bad debts based on receivable balances.

### **Management of Receivables**

* Accounts receivable turnover — Measures liquidity by comparing credit sales to average receivables.
* Average collection period — Average time receivables remain outstanding.
* Concentration of credit risk — Risk from heavy reliance on one customer or group.
* Other receivables — Nontrade receivables (interest, tax refunds, etc.).
